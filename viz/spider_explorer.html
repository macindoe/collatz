Spider explorer

A spider (spiders.md §19) is an abdomen (a comb node of depth ≥ 2, comb.md §18) together with every hair growing from one of its legs — the depth-1 children of the abdomen. A hair is a chain of depth-1 nodes: a link (core ≡ 2 mod 3) always continues to a fork (core ≡ 1 mod 3, Lemma 19.1.2); a fork continues, to a fork or a link by its core mod 9, exactly when its own t = v₃(4Ω−1) equals 1, and ends the hair when t ≥ 2 (Lemma 19.1.3). Every comb node belongs to exactly one spider or to the root's own piece, {(1,1)} (Theorem 19.2.2, the owner map). A spider's child spiders are the abdomens reached by one more comb edge from any of its nodes: the abdomen's own a = 1 door child and top-door child when they are not legs, and every hair fork's cascade child (always an abdomen) and, when it ends the hair (t ≥ 2), its top-door child too (Definition 19.3.1). Type a state or a positive odd integer and click go to open the spider that owns it; click a parent spider or child spider link to move one spider at a time. The vocabulary is spiders.md's own: abdomen, leg, hair, fork, link, orphan link, hair fork, spider, spider tree.
