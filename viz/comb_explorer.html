Comb explorer

The peak comb (comb.md §18) is the backward tree of reduced states (ω, d), each node placed by its peak A = 3dω − 1 = 2s·y, read as branch s on the door y (18.1). A node at branch s ≥ 3 hangs from the node at branch s − 2 on the same door by a cascade edge (the peak divided by 4; 18.2.1, Lemma 18.2.5); the node at the lowest admissible branch hangs from the door's own state, one step of F, by a door edge. Read upward, every node has one cascade child and one door child per live door (Proposition 18.2.3): the top door 2dω − 1 is dead when it is a multiple of 3, the side doors never are (reverse.md 14.5.1). λ is the level, the number of edges to (1,1); η is the number of F-steps, and η = 1 + the door edges on the path (Proposition 18.3.2). The tree starts at the root (1,1), whose only child is (1,2). The + control expands a node by one level, and nothing is computed before it is shown; clicking a card opens it in the panel: its path to the root, the raw descent from any of its representatives through the peaks of the lower siblings on its door (18.4), and the chain of siblings. Every integer on this page is exact; integers longer than 24 digits are abbreviated and show in full when clicked.

    Levels double from about level 17 (18.5; census counts to level 27 shown beside each λ); the largest peak at level λ is 22λ+1, on door 1 (Lemma 18.5.1).
