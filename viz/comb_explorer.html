Comb explorer

The peak comb (comb.md §18) is the backward tree of reduced states (ω, d), each node placed by its peak A = 3dω − 1 = 2s·y, read as branch s on the door y (18.1). A node at branch s ≥ 3 hangs from the node at branch s − 2 on the same door by a cascade edge (the peak divided by 4; 18.2.1, Lemma 18.2.5); the node at the lowest admissible branch hangs from the door's own state, one step of F, by a door edge. Read upward, every node has one cascade child and one door child per live door (Proposition 18.2.3): the top door 2dω − 1 is dead when it is a multiple of 3, the side doors never are (reverse.md 14.5.1). λ is the level, the number of edges to the root; η is the number of F-steps. The tree starts at the root (1,1), whose only child is (1,2), and η = 1 + the door edges on the path (Proposition 18.3.2). The root selector opens the same construction on the negative odd integers (18.8), where the reduced map has three components, one per classical negative cycle: the singular root (−1,1), whose door parent state(−1) does not exist; the fixed state (−1,2); and the 2-cycle (−1,4) ↔ (−5,3), whose comb ring has three nodes. Every rule is the same; λ counts edges to the ring, η counts F-steps to the F-cycle (or to the singular door), and η − (door edges) is 0 or 1 (18.8.2). The + control expands a node by one level, and nothing is computed before it is shown; clicking a card opens it in the panel: its path to the ring, the raw descent from any of its representatives through the peaks of the lower siblings on its door (18.4), and the chain of siblings. Rows are indented by level up to level 24 and flat beyond (the level is printed on every card). Every integer on this page is exact; integers longer than 24 digits are abbreviated and show in full when clicked.
