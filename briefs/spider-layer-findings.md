**Item A.2's class split, resolved exactly.** The brief's own pre-check left open whether the near-even split of the fork's top-door child's class at `t = 1` (`27,515` links against `27,623` forks) is "exact by residue... or only statistical." It is **exact**: `Ω ≡ 1 (mod 9)` always gives a fork continuation, `Ω ≡ 4 (mod 9)` always gives a link (Lemma 19.1.3's proof, verified on `1,997` random forks with `0` exceptions at either residue). Since these are two residue classes of equal density (`1/18` each among valid cores), their expected frequencies in *any* finite window are equal; the brief's own near-`50/50` split (`27,515` vs `27,623`, a `0.2%` imbalance) is exactly the finite-sample noise this predicts, not a separate statistical phenomenon needing its own explanation.

**Item A.4, the fork parent-depth table — not pre-checked in the brief, derived here in full.** The brief handed over a specific claimed formula for the fork case ("parent of depth `1` iff `Ω ≡ 1` or `3 (mod 8)`") with a proof sketch, explicitly flagging it as *not numerically pre-checked*. Verified on `2,973` random depth-1 states spanning every one of the `8` residue cells, `0` failures; the CRT-to-`24` residues (`{1, 17, 19}` for the non-legs) checked directly.

## What the pre-check's own numbers turned out to mean (Item B)

The most consequential finding of this session is not a defect in the brief's pre-checked numbers — every one of them reproduced exactly (`247,932` nodes, `82,491` pieces, mean `3.006`, largest `40`; `96,620` hairs; the length distribution `53,145, 28,290, 8,818, …` down to the isolated `13:1, 15:1`) — but a finding about what those numbers *measure*. The hair-length distribution, taken directly from the level-`18` census, disagrees with the derived Markov-chain law by a factor of nearly `6` at length `1` (measured `55.0%` against a predicted `9.5%`). Tracing this down: a hair node discovered exactly at level `18` carries no census-recorded child (the census stops building children past that level), so any hair reaching the boundary is cut off there whether or not it truly continues. By Lemma 19.1.2 a link's continuation is not a rate but a certainty — so every one of the `34,743` link legs sitting exactly at level `18` (half the link-leg population) was silently recorded as a length-`1` hair. Recomputing every hair's length by unbounded ground-truth arithmetic (`comb_children`, no census dependency) shows `69,099` of the `96,620` hairs (`71.5%`) are affected, and the corrected distribution matches the predicted law to within `2%` at every one of the first eight lengths (table in spiders.md 19.2). The true maximum hair length in this census is `55`, not the window's `15`.

This is recorded as a correction to the *reading* of the brief's pre-check, not to its numbers: the brief's own figures are reproduced exactly (they are, after all, a direct measurement of the same truncated window), and spiders.md keeps them for that reason, but the law is tested — and confirmed — only after removing the boundary artifact. The general lesson, consistent with `briefs/comb-signatures-findings.md`'s Revision 1 standard (depth-confounded comparisons, tautological baselines): a finite census window can silently misrepresent an unbounded-length statistic even when every individual count in the window is correct, and the fix is not a different sample but a ground-truth recomputation of the specific quantity being tested.

## A hand-derivation error caught by the code

The findings section asked for the spider of `(1,2)` "written out by hand... and matched." The first hand pass concluded `2` child spiders (`(19,3)` from `(43,1)`'s cascade child, `(1,4)` from `(7,1)`'s cascade child), missing that both `(43,1)` and `(7,1)` have `t ≥ 2` — meaning their *top-door* children are **also** abdomens (depth `t`, not depth `1`), each spawning a second new spider (`(19,2)` and `(1,3)` respectively). ## The reading of Item C

**The residue-modulus measurement.** Following comb.md 18.9.2.2–18.9.3's method (a sufficient digit budget composed edge by edge, refined by an exact full-coset search), a spider's *whole shape* — not its `k`-level signature, but every hair's exact length and class sequence, to termination — needs a budget that composes the same way: `0` cost per link step (a cascade edge with `d′ = D(v) = 1`), `1` digit per fork-continuation step (a door-`a=0` edge with `d′ = 1`), and the base cap `J = 2` (the abdomen/depth-1 boundary) at every point the recursion stops. Measured over `2,500` sampled spiders (cap `J = 2`): mean sufficient budget `4.97`, max `26`, not showing a strong trend against spider size over the sampled range — because most sampled spiders are short (mean size `3`), and the budget is dominated by the base cap plus one or two hair steps for a typical spider; the dependence on size that *is* present (19.3's Pearson correlation on the child-spider count, `r = 0.82`) shows up in the tail, not the bulk. On the `120` spiders with sufficient budget `≤ 8`, the exact modulus (full-coset search) matched the sufficient one exactly in every case — no slack found on the tested range, unlike 18.9.3's own `χ − ψ ∈ {0,1}` measurement for signatures; the difference is not a contradiction, since the two budgets are different objects (a whole spider's shape vs. a `k`-capped signature) composed by the same method.

**The child-spider question.** Answered **no**, with the structural reason asked for rather than a naming: within every one of `45` tested `(D, Ω mod 9)` classes of size `≥ 5`, the child-spider count varies (e.g. `{0, 2, 4}` within one class), and it correlates with spider size (`r = 0.82` over the whole census). The reason is that the count is `Σ (1 + [t ≥ 2])` over every fork in the spider's hairs, and the number of forks in a spider grows with its hair lengths — themselves unbounded, per 19.2's own finding. This is the same phenomenon as comb.md 18.9.2's digit budget growing with the depth asked of a signature, applied here along a hair instead of down a fixed number of comb levels.
