## 1. The claim in the literature's terms, and the dictionary used

**Dictionary** (TOUR.md's row for periods 1–2, checked and corrected here by `experiments/ceiling_novelty_check.py`):

| Record | Simons–de Weger 2005 (SdW) | Halbeisen–Hungerbühler 1997 (HH) |
|---|---|---|
| period `p` (blocks) | `m`, the number of local minima = maximal runs of odd numbers in the Terras orbit (`T(x) = (3x+1)/2`, `x/2`) — **`m = p` exactly**, every block being `1^{m_t} 0^{s_t}` with `s_t ≥ 1` (§4.3) | not a parameter |
| `n = Σ m_t` (odd steps) | `K` | `n` (number of 1s) |
| `K = Σ s_t + n` (halvings) | `K + L` | `l` (length) |
| `q = 2^K − 3^n` | `Δ = 2^{K+L} − 3^K` | `2^l − 3^n` |
| `Λ = K log 2 − n log 3`, `γ = K − log₂ q` (`Λ ≈ 2^{−γ}`) | `Λ = (K+L) log 2 − K log 3` | — |
| entry depth `m_t`; size condition `q ≤ R_r` at rotation `r` | `k_i`; `a_i ≥ 1` where `x_i = 2^{k_i} a_i − 1` — **`a_t = R_t/q` exactly** at every block start (checked, §4.3) | rational cycle element `≥ 1`; `φ(s)/(2^l − 3^n)` |
| a configuration meeting every size condition ("size-passer") | a rational solution of the matrix equation with all `a_i ≥ 1` ("rational solutions with common denominator `q` correspond to solutions of the `3n + q`-problem", footnote, p. 57) | a positive rational Collatz cycle with minimum `≥ 1` |
| `L = log₂ 3` | `δ` | `log₂ 3` |

**Part 1, restated.** For every nontrivial cycle with `m` local minima and `K` odd terms, `Λ` is bounded above by a quantity of the form `C(m)·2^{−((δ−1)/(δ^m−1))·K}`; against an effective lower bound for `Λ` (Rhin) this bounds `K` by `O(m·δ^m)` for every `m`.

**Part 2, restated.** That exponential-in-`m` loss is intrinsic to size arguments: for every `m` there are configurations (parity patterns with `m` odd runs) satisfying every `a_i ≥ 1` with `K ≍ δ^m` and `Λ` bounded below by an absolute constant — so no size-only bound can replace `(δ−1)/(δ^m−1)` by anything decaying slower in `m`, and uniform exclusion needs the divisibility (integrality) of the `a_i`.

## 4. The two parts in detail

### 4.1 Part 1 — subsumed

The implication, written out. SdW Lemma 7 (p. 58) holds for every `m`-cycle whose minima satisfy `x_i ≥ X₀`; its proof uses only the chain equation, `a_i ≥ 1`, and `x_i ≥ X₀` (the last only through `b = 2^{−1/δ}(1 + X₀^{−1})`, "negligible", p. 59). With `m = p` (§4.3), `K_SdW = n`, and `Λ = −ln(1 − 2^{−γ}) ∈ (2^{−γ}, 2·2^{−γ})` for `γ > 1`, Lemma 7 gives

```text
γ  >  (L−1)·n/(L^p − 1) − log₂(p·c_p),
```

(from `2^{−γ} < Λ`), which is Theorem 12.8.1 (`γ + log₂ p > (L−1)n/(L^p − 1)`) up to the additive constant `log₂ c_p` (`c_p < 0.30577` for `p ≥ 30` at their `X₀`, so their form is the slightly stronger one there). Combined with Rhin (their Lemma 12, the very bound `cycles.md` 12.5.3 takes from them), Lemma 14 and Theorem 3(d) give `K < K₁(m)` with `K₁(m) ~ 15.107·m·δ^m` — Corollary 12.8.2's `n₀(p) = O(p·L^p)`, explicitly, for every `m`. The record's own observation that `n₀(p)/(p·1.585^p)` "falls to ~20 by p = 100 (still decreasing)" is SdW's `k₁(m)` decreasing to `15.107…`.

What the record has that SdW do not: a different proof route (the rotation numerators `R_r` and a max-plus recursion, against SdW's chaining `x_{i+1} < b^δ x_i^δ` of the minima), stated without the verification threshold. Neither is a novelty claim: the proof printed on p. 59 runs unchanged at `X₀ = 1` with a different constant. **Verdict: subsumed.** Earlier still, Brox 2000 (cited) gives finiteness for each `m`, effective in principle but not explicit.

### 4.2 Part 2 — partially anticipated

**Anticipated:**
1. *The statement, as an expectation, in the same parameter and about the same inequality:* SdW p. 69 (quoted in §3). It rests on "computational and heuristic evidence", displays no configuration, and proves nothing. Simons 2022 (p. 150) improves only Lemma 7's prefactor, which is consistent with it.
2. *The phenomenon, proved, in the length parameter:* HH 1997 prove that the size-only criterion is optimal (§4.4, p. 236), that it is attained by an explicit extremal configuration at every length (Lemma 5, p. 230), that size arguments therefore "cannot" exclude cycles (p. 238), and that the remaining condition is the rotation-gcd condition (A′) (p. 238) — which is `q | R_r` at every rotation. So **Theorem 4.6's closing sentence ("Uniform cycle exclusion therefore requires the divisibility system") is anticipated by HH 1997 in the length frame**, with proof. Belaga 2003 (cited) proves a sharp perigee bound in the same frame.

**Not anticipated (not found in this search):**
- a proof, in the local-minima parameter `m` (= `p`), that the exponential dependence of Lemma 7 / the trim is attained;

**Exactly what the closest prior statement (SdW p. 69) lacks:** a proof; a configuration; any constant. Its quantifier is "for given m and K the worst case values for k_i and l_i", i.e. over their matrix equation with the minima `≥ X₀`. **One scope gap on our side, to be stated rather than smoothed:** the record proves sharpness at threshold `1` (`q ≤ R_r`, i.e. `a_i ≥ 1`, rational elements `≥ 1`), not at SdW's verification threshold `x_min ≥ X₀`. The shape (exponential in `m`) is the same and a version at threshold `X₀` would cost about `log₂ X₀` bits of `γ`, but that version is not in the record and is not checked here.

The script's Part B makes the comparison concrete on one 12.8.6.2 staircase per `p = 8..18`: every one passes all size tests in both dictionaries, SdW Lemma 7 holds on each, and Lemma 7's exponent `(δ−1)n/(δ^m − 1)` stays between `0.586` and `0.659` along the family, while `log₂ Λ` stays between `−8.3` and `−3.7`. So Lemma 7's `δ^{−m}` decay is met to within a constant in the exponent, as SdW expected.

### 4.3 Collateral: `cycles.md` 12.4's `m` is wrong

SdW define an `m`-cycle on the Terras map (p. 51): "the numbers in it appear in m sequences, each consisting of an increasing subsequence of odd numbers, followed by a decreasing subsequence of even numbers"; Lagarias II item 94 paraphrases it as "m local minima, i.e. … m blocks of consecutive odd integers". A reduced block is the Terras word `1^{m_t} 0^{s_t}` with `s_t ≥ 1`, so every block — including an `m_t = 1` block, whose start `x` satisfies `2x > x < (3x+1)/2` — is exactly one maximal odd run. Hence **SdW's `m` = `p`**, and the record's `m = #{t : m_t ≥ 2} ≤ p` (12.4, line 74) is an undercount. The "pure fall" reading of an `m_t = 1` block is true in the odd-to-odd trajectory, but SdW count in the Terras trajectory. It also agrees with Brox's descents (Lagarias II: "This is equal to the number of descents in the terminology of Brox (2000)"), since each block ends in exactly one descent. Checked: Part A (the published `p = 7` staircase, `m_t = (4,7,9,15,23,35,1)`): odd runs `= 7`, the 12.4 formula `= 6`; Part B: SdW `m = p` at every `p = 8..18`, the 12.4 formula smaller at every one.

Consequences: 12.4's "in particular no cycle of period `p ≤ M₀` exists" stands, and so does publication.md's "crossover threshold `p > 91` correctly calibrated". The clause "any cycle whatsoever must contain more than `M₀` deep-entry blocks" is unsupported. The TOUR.md dictionary row for periods 1–2 inherits nothing wrong. For this verdict the effect is that the subsumption of part 1 is exact (`m = p`), not merely implied by a stronger statement in a smaller parameter.
