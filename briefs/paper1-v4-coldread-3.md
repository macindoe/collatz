## 3. SUMMARY: the three worst remaining problems

1. The anchor is never made concrete for a non-specialist. The paper's central object (the anchor, the "fixed binary pattern that belongs to ω") is given in words only, with worked values only for ω = 1 (p8). "How d compares with the anchor" (p9), "the change in anchor" (p9) and "2-adic logarithms of whole numbers" (p17) follow without a plain-words definition, so "Where the difficulty is" (p10) and §7 cannot be followed by a reader without the appendices. One short worked example for a second ω (say ω = 3 or 5) and one plain sentence per term would fix most of this.

2. The two densest sentences sit where the reader has the least vocabulary: the abstract's fair-coin sentence (p1, item 7) and the §6 hypothesis (p15, item 49). Each packs five or six undefined terms (admissible, tolerance, finite word, recorded frequency, fixed power, budget) into one clause. A reader stops at both. The plain claim should come first, the formal wording second.

3. Several internal inconsistencies a careful reader will hit, all fixable in text: the halvings count in §4 versus the definition of s (item 16); the seven-block staircase's γ = 6.74 next to "γ at most about 5.14" on the next page (items 41, 45); "hence the row entered — but none for which skeleton is entered" (item 65); and the 62,937 count, which is 63 short of the stated definition (item 34). Plus the unfilled "[v4 version DOI]" placeholder on p1 and p2 (item 9).
