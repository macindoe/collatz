## The signed reduced map (Item A — definitions, re-derived)

For a nonzero odd integer `x ≠ −1`, write `x + 1 = 2^m 3^a Ω` with `Ω` odd, `3 ∤ Ω`, `Ω` of the sign of `x + 1`; the state is `(Ω, m + a)`, so negative odd `x < −1` have states with `Ω < 0`. The peak `A = 3^d ω − 1`, exit valuation `s = v₂(A)`, exit `y = A / 2^s` (exact signed division) and `F(ω,d) = state(y)` are the same formulas (spine.md §5.6); Theorem 14.15.6.2 gives sign preservation, so negative states map to negative states. The singular point: `x = −1` has `x + 1 = 0` and no state; a state whose exit is `−1` has no `F`-image — precisely `(−1, 1)`, whose peak is `−4`, `s = 2`, exit `−1`. Doors, admissible branches (`s` odd iff `y ≡ 1 (mod 3)`, with residues taken in `{0,1,2}`: `−5 ≡ 1`, `−7 ≡ 2`), the predecessor rule 14.1.1, mortality 14.5.1, the cascade step 14.10.1, the comb parent rule 18.2.1 and the children rule 18.2.3 all carry over verbatim — **prove this**, clause by clause, noting exactly where a sign could have entered (the exact division by `2^s`, the recovery of `Ω`, the residue convention) and why it does not.

## The three components and their roots (Item B)

The signed `F`-graph on negative states has (at least) three components, one per known cycle; the empirical negative conjecture is that every negative odd integer's orbit reaches one of the three (**measured in the pre-check:** every odd `x` in `(−2·10^5, −1)` does — `32,880` to `−1`, `32,172` to the `−5` cycle, `34,947` to the `−17` cycle, none elsewhere; re-measure to `10^6` and record it as a measurement, not a claim). In each component the comb is the parent graph of 18.2.1 with its ring removed:

1. **The singular component.** Root `(−1,1)`: it is the branch-`2` node on the singular door `−1` (`−1 ≡ 2 (mod 3)`, so even branches), its door parent does not exist, and that missing edge plays the role of the positive root's excluded self-loop. Door `−1`'s chain at branches `2, 4, 6, 8, 10` is `(−1,1), (−5,1), (−7,2), (−85,1), (−341,1)`, with depth `d = v₃(2^s − 1) = 1 + v₃(s)` for even `s` — Lemma 14.2.1, the `y = −1` case 14.2 already treats. `(−1,1)` has one door, `−3`, dead, so **the root has exactly one child**, `(−5,1)`, by a cascade edge — the mirror of the positive root's single child `(1,2)`.
2. **The fixed-state component.** Root `(−1,2)`: exit `−5` at `s = 1`; its door `−5` (`a = 0`) at branch `1` gives `(−1,2)` itself — the excluded self-loop; its door `−7` (`a = 1`, `≡ 2`) at branch `2` gives `(−1,3)`; its cascade child is `(−13,1)` at branch `3` on door `−5`. **The root has two children.**
3. **The 2-cycle component.** The `F`-ring is `(−1,4) ↔ (−5,3)`, but the **comb ring has three nodes**: `s(−1,4) = 1`, so its parent is the door parent `(−5,3)`; `s(−5,3) = 3` on door `−17`, so its parent is the *cascade* parent, the branch-`1` node on door `−17`, which is `(−11,1)`; and `(−11,1)` has `s = 1` with door parent `(−1,4)`. So the parent map cycles `(−1,4) → (−5,3) → (−11,1) → (−1,4)`, one node per comb edge of the period: in general an `F`-cycle of period `p` with exit valuations `s_i` becomes a parent-graph cycle of length `Σ_i (1 + ⌊(s_i − 1)/2⌋)` (Lemma 18.7.1's count over one period). **Prove this general statement** and apply it: the ring is the set of nodes whose parent chain never leaves the ring; the component's comb is every other node with its parent, and the level `λ` is the number of parent edges to the ring. Children of ring nodes that are not ring nodes are level `1` (e.g. `(−109,1)`, `(−11,2)`, `(−49,1)`, `(−73,1)` below `(−1,4)`; `(−181,1)`, `(−1,5)`, `(−121,1)` below `(−5,3)`; and `(−11,1)`'s non-ring children).

For the positive comb the corresponding statements are 18.2.2–18.2.3; state the three negative cases in the same form, each with its ring, and prove that within each component the parent map on non-ring nodes is a tree onto the ring (the argument of 18.2.2(c) with "reaches `(1,1)`" replaced by "reaches the ring" or "reaches the singular door").

## Where the sign enters (Item D — one paragraph, exact)
