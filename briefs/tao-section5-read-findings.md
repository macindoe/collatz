## 0. Verdict

**The conditional edit is not applied.** Q1 comes out *no* on the mechanism the brief supposed, and Q2 comes out **(c)**: the whole-descent letter statistics are not in Tao verbatim and are not an immediate corollary of his displayed statements; they would need an argument he does not give, whose shape is visible in Section 3 and whose error accounting closes on paper, but which is not on the page. The brief's condition (Q1 and Q2 both (a) or (b)) is therefore not met, and `publication.md` and `aeh.md` are untouched on this branch. Proposed before/after wording is carried in §7 with the uncertainty stated.

One-line answers:

- **Q1.** No. Proposition 1.9 is applied exactly once per scale, at (5.4) (p. 20), to a *fresh* logarithmically uniform draw `N_y ≡ Log(2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α])` — never to a first-passage law or a push-forward — with `n = n_0 = ⌊log x/(10 log 2)⌋` Syracuse steps on `n' = 3n_0` bits of uniformity; that is inside the classical budget by a factor of three, and one such window is 20–41 stages deep. The push-forward is controlled only through (1.20), whose engine is 3-adic (Proposition 1.14), not 2-adic uniformity.
- **Q2.** (c). Per stage, for the fresh variable, the pattern statistics are one standard concentration step away from (5.4) — that step is `aeh.md` 13.2.4(d)'s, in Syracuse coordinates, over a window whose endpoints are stopping times pinned by (5.15). The transfer across stages and the whole-descent assembly are not displayed; Section 3 transfers exactly one event (`Syr_min ≤ N_0`) and draws exactly one conclusion. The accounting, if the missing steps were supplied: a union bound of `O(log^{-c} N_0)` over the stages down to altitude `N_0`, vanishing as `N_0 → ∞` and not as `x → ∞` at fixed `N_0` — Theorem 1.3's shape exactly.
- **Q3.** Nothing past the first window is delivered by a displayed statement. If the (c)-argument were carried out: (i) logarithmic density on `[y, y^α]`, strictly weaker than 13.2.1's natural density on `[N, 2N)`; (ii) the Syracuse-to-block conversion would *not* bite for frequencies, because the letter dictionary of 13.6.5 makes block letters finite valuation patterns, but the horizon parametrisation differs (altitude-indexed stages versus a rate `θ`); (iii) valuation words and `(m, r)` letters are interchangeable at the level of all-finite-pattern frequencies; (iv) the budget clause `τ` has no counterpart in Tao's proof, which never tallies past a fresh window's budget; (v) the depth marginal would follow through the wiki's own 13.6.4 (⇒), since capped depths are finite letter patterns — Tao's `Syrac` laws are laws over starts at fixed time, not along-orbit frequencies.
- **Q4.** Remark 1.16 (p. 12) asks for fine-scale mixing of the whole random affine map `Aff_{Geom(2)^n}` — multiplier and offset jointly — instead of the offset `F_n(Geom(2)^n)` alone; this is what would let the stabilisation run from a natural-density-uniform start, whose multiplicative non-invariance is the stated reason for logarithmic density (pp. 2, 7). Inselmann's `∗`-density, per the record (`briefs/v3r3-inselmann-horizon-findings.md` §1.8, item 1 of its open list), supplies the envelope and the parity first moment at natural density over one push-forward, and no pattern of length ≥ 2; it does not supply the distributional statement and does not iterate stages.
- **Q5.** Three corrections to the brief's Section 1 reading, none fatal to its shape: Proposition 1.9 is applied to fresh draws, not to "the current law"; one application covers 20–41 stages, not one; and Section 3's iteration is a (1.20)-per-stage iteration of a single event, not a Proposition-1.9-per-stage iteration. The brief's gluing claim (Prop 1.11 via Prop 1.14) is correct as stated (p. 12).

## 1. What Tao's argument does, in his words

The chain of statements, with the places they are made.

**Logarithmic density, and why.** p. 2: "For technical reasons, the notion of 'almost all' that we will use here is based on logarithmic density, which has better approximate multiplicative invariance properties than the more familiar notion of natural density". p. 7, after (1.17): "We remark that the multiplicative inaccuracy of `exp(O(n^{1/2}))` in (1.17) is the main reason why we work with logarithmic density instead of natural density in this paper".

**The non-iteration obstruction, and the device.** p. 2: "It is tempting to try to iterate these results to lower the value of `θ` further. However, one runs into the difficulty that the uniform (or logarithmic) measure does not enjoy any invariance properties with respect to the Collatz map: in particular, even if it is true that `Col_min(N) < x^θ` for almost all `N ∈ [1, x]`, and `Col_min(N') ≤ x^{θ²}` for almost all `N' ∈ [1, x^θ]`, the two claims cannot be immediately concatenated to imply that `Col_min(N) ≤ x^{θ²}` for almost all `N ∈ [1, x]`, since the Collatz iteration may send almost all of `[1, x]` into a very sparse subset of `[1, x^θ]`, and in particular into the exceptional set of the latter claim". Then: "Nevertheless, in this paper we show that it is possible to locate an alternate probability measure (or more precisely, a family of probability measures) on the natural numbers with enough invariance properties that an iterative argument does become fruitful." p. 8: "In our context, we do not expect to have any useful invariant probability measures for the dynamics due to the geometric decay (1.16) ... Instead, we can construct a family of probability measures `ν_x` which are approximately transported to each other by certain iterations of the Syracuse map (by a variable amount of time)."

**Proposition 1.9 (p. 6).** "Let `n ∈ N`, and let `N` be a random variable taking values in `2N+1`. Suppose there exist an absolute constant `c_0 > 0` and some natural number `n' ≥ (2 + c_0)n` such that `N mod 2^{n'}` is approximately uniformly distributed in the odd residue classes `(2Z+1)/2^{n'}Z` of `Z/2^{n'}Z`, in the sense that `d_TV(N mod 2^{n'}, Unif((2Z+1)/2^{n'}Z)) ≪ 2^{-n'}` (1.11). Then `d_TV(a^{(n)}(N), Geom(2)^n) ≪ 2^{-c_1 n}` (1.12) for some absolute constant `c_1 > 0` (depending on `c_0`)." Its proof (Section 4, pp. 18–20) is the classical cylinder count: the event `a^{(n)}(N) = a` pins `N` to one odd class mod `2^{|a|+1}`, of probability `2^{-|a|} + O(2^{-n'})` when `|a| < n'` (p. 20, the display before (4.1)), and the tail `|a| ≥ n'` has probability `O(2^{-cn})` by Lemma 4.1 (p. 18), a Stirling/Chernoff count. This is `aeh.md` 13.2.4(a)–(b) in Syracuse coordinates, with `n' ≥ (2 + c_0)n` playing the consistency clause.

**Where Proposition 1.9 is applied, announced (pp. 6–7).** "We will apply this proposition in Section 5, not to the original logarithmic distribution `Log(2N+1 ∩ [1, x])` (which has too heavy a tail near 1 for the hypothesis (1.11) to apply), but to the variant `Log(2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α])` for some large `y` and some `α > 1` close to 1."

**The range of one window (p. 8, §1.3).** "Roughly speaking, Proposition 1.9 lets one obtain good control on the Syracuse iterates `Syr^n(N)` for almost all `N` and for times `n` up to `c log N` for a small absolute constant `c`. This already can be used in conjunction with a rigorous version of (1.16) or (1.17) to recover the previously mentioned result `Syr_min(N) ≤ N^{1−c}` for almost all `N` and some absolute constant `c > 0`; see Section 5 for details." And Remark 5.1 (pp. 21–22): "This argument already establishes that `Syr_min(N) ≤ N^θ` for almost all `N` for any `θ > 1/α`; by optimising the numerical exponents in this argument one can eventually recover the results of Korec [9] mentioned in the introduction."

**Proposition 1.11 (pp. 8–9), the gluing statement.** With `T_x(N) := inf{n ∈ N : Syr^n(N) ≤ x}`, `Pass_x(N) := Syr^{T_x(N)}(N)` (convention `Syr^∞(N) := 1`), and `α := 1.001` (1.18): "For any `y` with `2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α]` is non-empty (and in particular, for any sufficiently large `y`), let `N_y` be a random variable with distribution `N_y ≡ Log(2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α])`. Then for sufficiently large `x`, we have the estimates `P(T_x(N_y) = +∞) ≪ x^{-c}` (1.19) for `y = x^α, x^{α²}`, and also `d_TV(Pass_x(N_{x^α}), Pass_x(N_{x^{α²}})) ≪ log^{-c} x` (1.20) for some absolute constant `c > 0`."

**What (1.20) is for (p. 9).** "Informally, this theorem asserts that the Syracuse orbits of `N_{x^α}` and `N_{x^{α²}}` are almost indistinguishable from each other once they pass `x`, as long as one synchronises the orbits so that they simultaneously pass `x` for the first time. In Section 3 we shall see how Theorem 1.6 (and hence Theorem 1.3) follows from Proposition 1.11; basically the point is that (1.19), (1.20) imply that the first passage map `Pass_x` approximately maps the distribution `ν_{x^α}` of `Pass_{x^α}(N_{x^{α²}})` to the distribution `ν_x` of `Pass_x(N_{x^α})`, and one can then iterate this to map almost all of the probabilistic mass of `N_y` for large `y` to be arbitrarily close to the bounded state `N = O(1)`. The implication is very general and does not use any particular properties of the Syracuse map beyond (1.19), (1.20)." And: "We remark that the bound of `O(log^{-c} x)` in (1.20) is stronger than is needed for this argument; any bound of the form `O((log log x)^{-1-c})` would have sufficed."

**The engine (p. 12).** "In Section 5, we show how Proposition 1.11 (and hence Theorem 1.3) follows from Proposition 1.14 and Proposition 1.9." Proposition 1.14 (p. 11) is the fine-scale 3-adic mixing of `Syrac(Z/3^nZ) ≡ F_n(Geom(2)^n) mod 3^n` (1.22).

**Section 3, the iteration (pp. 16–18).** Theorem 3.1: "For `N_0 ≥ 2` and `x ≥ 2`, one has `(1/log x) Σ_{N ∈ 2N+1 ∩ [1,x] : Syr_min(N) > N_0} 1/N ≪ 1/log^c N_0`". The proof defines `E_{N_0} := {N ∈ 2N+1 : Syr_min(N) ≤ N_0}` and "Let `B_x = B_{x,N_0}` denote the event that `T_x(N_{x^α}) < +∞` and `Pass_x(N_{x^α}) ∈ E_{N_0}`" (p. 17); the one-stage step is the monotonicity "`Syr_min(Pass_{x^α}(N_{x^{α²}})) ≤ Syr_min(Pass_x(N_{x^{α²}})) ≤ N_0`. In particular, the event `B_{x^α}` holds in this case. From this, (1.19), and (1.20), (1.10) we have `P(B_{x^α}) ≥ ... ≥ P(B_x) − O(log^{-c} x)`" (p. 17), with "(note that the `O(x^{-c})` error can be absorbed into the `O(log^{-c} x)` term)". The stages: "Let `J = J(x, N_0)` be the first natural number such that the quantity `y := x^{α^{-J}}` is less than `N_0^{1/α}`. ... `P(B_{y^{α^{j−1}}}) ≥ P(B_{y^{α^{j−2}}}) − O((α^j log y)^{-c})` for all `j = 1, ..., J`" (p. 17); "Summing the telescoping series, we conclude that `P(B_{y^{α^{J−1}}}) ≥ 1 − O(log^{-c} y)` ... so `P(B_{x^{1/α}}) ≥ 1 − O(log^{-c} N_0)`" (p. 18). Then the covering: "Covering the interval `2N+1 ∩ [1, x]` by intervals of the form `2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α]` for various `y`, we obtain the claim" (p. 18), and the passage to Theorem 1.6: "Since `1/log^c f̃(x)` goes to zero as `x → ∞`, we conclude from telescoping series that the set `{N ∈ 2N+1 : Syr_min(N) > f(N)}` has zero logarithmic density" (p. 18).

**Section 5, the one application of Proposition 1.9 (p. 20).** "Let `x` be sufficiently large. We take `y` to be either `x^α` or `x^{α²}`. ... Now introduce the quantities `n_0 := ⌊log x/(10 log 2)⌋` (5.1) (so that `2^{n_0} ≍ x^{0.1}`) and `m_0 := ⌊(α−1)/100 · log x⌋` (5.2). Since the random variable `N_y` takes values in `[y, y^α]`, we see from (1.18) that we would expect the bounds `m_0 ≤ T_x(N_y) ≤ n_0` (5.3) to hold with high probability." Then: "Since `N_y ≡ Log(2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α])`, a routine application of the integral test reveals that `d_TV(N_y mod 2^{3n_0}, Unif((2Z+1)/2^{3n_0}Z)) ≪ 2^{-3n_0}` (with plenty of room to spare), hence by Proposition 1.9 `d_TV(a^{(n_0)}(N_y), Geom(2)^{n_0}) ≪ 2^{-cn_0}` (5.4)."

**Section 5, the proof of (1.20) (pp. 22–28).** "By (1.10), it suffices to show that for `E ⊂ 2N+1 ∩ [1, x]`, that `P(Pass_x(N_y) ∈ E) = (1 + O(log^{-c} x))Q + O(log^{-c} x)` (5.7) for some quantity `Q` that can depend on `x, α, E` but is independent of whether `y` is equal to `x^α` or `x^{α²}`" (p. 22). "The strategy is to manipulate the left-hand side of (5.7) into an expression that involves the Syracuse random variables `Syrac(Z/3^nZ)` for various `n` (in a range `I_y` depending on `y`) plus a small error, and then appeal to Proposition 1.14 to remove the dependence on `n` and hence on `y` in the main term. The main difficulty is that the first passage location `Pass_x(N_y)` involves a first passage time `n = T_x(N_y)` whose value is not known in advance; but by stepping back in time by a fixed number of steps `m_0`, we will be able to express the left-hand side of (5.7) (up to negligible errors) without having to explicitly refer to the first passage time." (p. 22). Proposition 5.2 (pp. 22–23) is the approximate formula (5.8) with the interval `I_y` (5.9), the set `E'` (5.10) of `M` with `T_x(M) = m_0` and `Pass_x(M) ∈ E`, and the set `A^{(n')}` (5.11) of tuples with `|a_{[1,n]} − 2n| < log^{0.6} x` for all `n ≤ n'`. Its proof uses (5.4) once more: "From (5.4), (1.10), and Lemma 2.2 we see that for every `0 ≤ n ≤ n_0`, one has `P(|a_{[1,n]} − 2n| ≥ log^{0.6} x) ≪ exp(−c log^{0.2} x)`. Hence ... `P(a^{(n_0)}(N_y) ∉ A^{(n_0)}) ≪ log^{-10} x` (5.12)" (p. 23); on that event "`Syr^n(N_y) = exp(O(log^{0.6} x))(3/4)^n N_y` (5.14) for all `0 ≤ n ≤ n_0`, which can be viewed as a rigorous version of the heuristic (1.17)" and "`T_x(N_y) = log(N_y/x)/log(4/3) + O(log^{0.6} x)` (5.15)" (p. 23), giving "`P(T_x(N_y) ∈ I_y) = 1 − O(log^{-c} x)` (5.16)" (p. 24). The main term is reduced to `Z := Σ_{M ∈ E'} 3^{m_0} P(M = Syrac(Z/3^{m_0}Z) mod 3^{m_0})/M` (5.21), "which would give the desired estimate (5.7) since `Z` does not depend on whether `y` is equal to `x^α` or `x^{α²}`" (p. 26), and the `y`-dependence is removed at the single invocation "Applying Proposition 1.14, Lemma 5.3 and the triangle inequality" (p. 28).

## 2. Q1 — is Proposition 1.9 applied at each stage to the current law?

**No.** The paper says where it applies Proposition 1.9 (pp. 6–7, quoted above) and does it exactly once per fresh scale, at (5.4) (p. 20), to `N_y ≡ Log(2N+1 ∩ [y, y^α])`. Within Section 5 the only other use of (5.4) is inside the proof of Proposition 5.2 (p. 23), on the same fresh `N_y`. Proposition 1.9 is never applied to `Pass_x(N_y)`, to `ν_x`, or to any push-forward; no statement in Sections 3 or 5 asserts or uses 2-adic near-uniformity of a first-passage law. The push-forward is handled by (1.20), a total-variation comparison between two first-passage laws *at the same* `x` from two fresh scales, and the mechanism of (1.20) is 3-adic: Proposition 5.2 rewrites `P(Pass_x(N_y) ∈ E)` as a sum over `Syrac(Z/3^{n−m_0}Z)` for `n ∈ I_y`, and Proposition 1.14 removes the `n`-dependence (pp. 22, 26, 28).

**What `n` is, relative to the scale.** `n_0 = ⌊log x/(10 log 2)⌋ = 0.1 log₂ x` Syracuse steps, with uniformity used to `n' = 3n_0 = 0.3 log₂ x` bits (p. 20; `c_0 = 1` in Proposition 1.9's `n' ≥ (2 + c_0)n`). The start `N_y` lies in `[y, y^α] ⊂ [x^α, x^{α³}]`, so in bits of the start `n'/log₂ N_y ≤ 0.3/α = 0.2997`. In `aeh.md`'s units: budget rate `τ ≤ 0.30`, and `n_0` Syracuse steps is about `n_0/2` blocks, a block horizon rate `θ ≈ 0.05` per bit of start, with `4θ ≈ 0.20 < τ < 1`. Each application is inside the classical budget, as it must be — Proposition 1.9 *is* the classical budget argument (Section 4).

**How much of the descent one window covers.** A stage is the descent from `x^α` to `x`, a factor `x^{α−1} = x^{0.001}`; by (5.15) it takes `T_x(N_{x^α}) ∈ [0.0035, 0.0070] log x` Syracuse steps (for `N_y` at the bottom and top of `[x^α, x^{α²}]`). The window `n_0 = 0.144 log x` (natural log) descends by `(3/4)^{n_0} = x^{−0.0415}`, i.e. one window is between `20.7` and `41.5` stages deep. Tao's own reading of one window's reach is `Syr_min(N) ≤ N^θ` for `θ > 1/α` (Remark 5.1, quoted above) — one stage's worth of descent at almost-all confidence, which is the Korec-type range, and everything below that is reached through (1.20), not through Proposition 1.9.

**Section 3 checked too.** Section 3 contains no application of Proposition 1.9; its only inputs are (1.19), (1.20), (1.10) and the identity `Syr_min(Pass_{x^α}(N)) ≤ Syr_min(Pass_x(N))` (p. 17).

## 3. Q2 — does the argument yield near-`Geom(2)` empirical pattern frequencies along the whole descent?

### 3.1 The per-stage statements, exactly as displayed

For a fresh `N_y`, `y ∈ {x^α, x^{α²}}`, `x` large, with `n_0`, `m_0` from (5.1)–(5.2):

- (5.4): `d_TV(a^{(n_0)}(N_y), Geom(2)^{n_0}) ≪ 2^{-cn_0}` — the full joint law of the first `n_0` valuations, within `x^{-c}` of iid `Geom(2)`.
- (5.12): `P(a^{(n_0)}(N_y) ∉ A^{(n_0)}) ≪ log^{-10} x` — the running *sum* `a_{[1,n]}` stays within `log^{0.6} x` of `2n` for every `n ≤ n_0`.
- (5.14): on `A^{(n_0)}`, `Syr^n(N_y) = exp(O(log^{0.6} x))(3/4)^n N_y` for `0 ≤ n ≤ n_0` — the two-sided envelope over one window.
- (5.15)–(5.16): `T_x(N_y) = log(N_y/x)/log(4/3) + O(log^{0.6} x)`, and `P(T_x(N_y) ∈ I_y) = 1 − O(log^{-c} x)`.
- (1.19): `P(T_x(N_y) = +∞) ≪ x^{-c}`.
- (1.20): `d_TV(Pass_x(N_{x^α}), Pass_x(N_{x^{α²}})) ≪ log^{-c} x`.

No displayed statement in Sections 3 or 5 concerns the empirical frequency of a valuation pattern of length ≥ 1 along an orbit — not per stage, not per window, not for the whole descent. The word-level statements are (5.4) (a law, over starts) and (5.12) (a sum). The only along-orbit quantities that are displayed are the envelope (5.14) and the passage time (5.15).

### 3.2 What Section 3 transfers, and what it could transfer

Section 3 transfers one event, `E_{N_0} = {Syr_min ≤ N_0}`, and its chain (p. 17) uses a property specific to that event: it is monotone along the orbit (`Syr_min` of an earlier point is at most `Syr_min` of a later point). The conclusion drawn is Theorem 3.1 and nothing else.

Tao's sentence "The implication is very general and does not use any particular properties of the Syracuse map beyond (1.19), (1.20)" (p. 9) is the textual support for transferring *other* events about the first-passage location; it is a sentence, not a displayed lemma. The general fact it points at — for any `E ⊂ [1, x]` and any `k ≥ 1`, `P(Pass_x(N_{x^{α^k}}) ∈ E) = P(Pass_x(N_{x^α}) ∈ E) + O(log^{-c} x)` uniformly in `k` — would follow from (1.20), the exact identity `Pass_x = Pass_x ∘ Pass_{x^α}` (true with the `Syr^∞ := 1` convention), and the data-processing inequality, the errors at intermediate scales `x^{α^j}` summing to a geometric series `log^{-c} x · Σ_j α^{-jc} = O(log^{-c} x)`. Tao does not display this; he displays the `E_{N_0}` chain.

### 3.3 The bookkeeping the brief asked for

Assume, for the accounting only, that the two undisplayed inputs are available: (I) for the fresh `N_y`, the empirical frequency of a fixed finite pattern `w` over the segment of valuations between `T_{x^α}(N_y)` and `T_x(N_y)` is within `ε` of `2^{-|w|}` off an event of probability `O(x^{-c(ε)})` — the step from (5.4) is a concentration bound under `Geom(2)^{n_0}` on a window whose endpoints are stopping times pinned to `O(log^{0.6} x)` by (5.15), with the segment length `Θ((α−1) log x) ≫ log^{0.6} x`; this is `aeh.md` 13.2.4(d)'s McDiarmid step with random endpoints, standard but not on the page; and (II) the transfer of §3.2.

Then, with the top scale `x` and the bottom `N_0`:

- **Stages.** Scales `x_j = x^{α^{-j}}`, `j = 0, ..., J`, with `J` the first integer for which `x^{α^{-J}} < N_0^{1/α}` (p. 17), so `J = ⌈log(log x/log N_0)/log α⌉ ≈ 1000.5 · ln(log x/log N_0)`. The stage-`j` segment is the descent from `x_j^α` to `x_j`.
- **Per-stage error.** Stage-`j` goodness is an event about `Pass_{x_j^α}(N)`, hence transferable. Its failure probability under the top-scale `N_x` is at most the transfer cost `O(log^{-c} x_j)` (the sum of (1.20) errors between `x` and `x_j`, dominated by its smallest scale) plus the fresh-variable failure `O(x_j^{-c(ε)})` from (I) plus (1.19)'s `O(x_j^{-c})` — the power terms absorbed into the log-power term, as on p. 17.
- **Union over stages.** `Σ_{j=1}^{J} O(log^{-c} x_j) = O(log^{-c} x) · Σ_j α^{jc}`, a geometric series dominated by its last term `j = J`: `O(log^{-c} x_J) = O(log^{-c} N_0)`. This is Tao's own summation on p. 18 for `E_{N_0}`, with the same dominant term; his remark that `O((log log x)^{-1-c})` would have sufficed (p. 9) is the statement that the sum over stages needs only summable decay in the scale index.
- **Whole descent.** The whole-descent frequency is the length-weighted average of the stage frequencies plus boundary terms — patterns straddling stage boundaries, `O(|w|)` per stage, `O(|w| J)` in all against a total length `Θ(log x)` — so it is within `ε + o(1)` of `2^{-|w|}` off a set of logarithmic density `O_ε(log^{-c} N_0)` on `[x, x^α]`, and by the covering step of p. 18, on `[1, x]`.
- **Does it vanish?** As `N_0 → ∞`, yes; as `x → ∞` at fixed `N_0`, no — the bound is a function of the bottom scale only. Exactly Theorem 1.3's shape (`f(N) → ∞` arbitrarily slowly, no absolute constant; Remark 1.4, p. 3). At a fixed-rate horizon of `θ` blocks per bit, `θ < 1/β`, the per-stage envelope (5.14) puts the horizon's bottom at `N_0 = N^{1 − 2θ log₂(4/3)}` (checked: `θ = 1/β = 1.2047` gives exponent `1.0000`, the descent ceiling), the stage count is `J = O_θ(1)` (≈ `233` at `θ = 1/4`, `1773` at `θ = 1`), and the bound becomes `O_θ(log^{-c} N)`, vanishing as `N → ∞`.

### 3.4 Classification

**(c).** The conclusion is not stated anywhere in the paper — not in Theorems 1.3, 1.6, 3.1, not in Proposition 1.11, not in Remarks 1.4, 1.16, 5.1 — and it is not an immediate corollary of displayed statements, because (I) and (II) are not displayed and the assembly in §3.3 is not either. ## 4. Q3 — clause by clause against `aeh.md` 13.2.1

Reading for each clause: what a displayed statement of Tao's gives, and what the (c)-argument of §3 would give if carried out. Nothing past the first window is delivered by a displayed statement.

**(i) Logarithmic versus natural density.** Tao: logarithmic (Definition 1.2, p. 2; Theorem 1.3, p. 3), and the proof works on `[y, y^α]` with `Log` (Proposition 1.11), then covers `[1, x]` (p. 18). The (c)-argument would inherit this: exceptional logarithmic density `O(log^{-c} N_0)` on `[y, y^α]`. `13.2.1` asks for natural density on `[N, 2N)` at every `N`. The two are not the same statement and the direction is one-way: vanishing natural density on every dyadic window implies vanishing logarithmic density on `[1, x]`; the converse fails (a sparse subsequence of wholly bad dyadic windows has logarithmic density zero). `13.2.5`'s shell conversion consumes the dyadic-window form. **Verdict: not delivered; would be delivered in a weaker density.**

**(ii) Syracuse-step horizon versus block horizon.** Tao's stages are indexed by altitude (`x^{α^{-j}}`) and his clock is Syracuse steps (`T_x`); `13.2.1`'s horizon is `T_N = ⌈θ log₂ N⌉` blocks with budget `Λ_N = ⌈τ log₂ N⌉` divisions. The conversion issue recorded at `13.3.2`/`13.2.3` — that reading a Syracuse-step horizon in blocks per bit divides by `E[m] = 2`, which is the frequency of exponents `≥ 2`, a one-pattern statistic in Syracuse time — **would not bite** for the (c)-argument, because its per-stage content is all finite valuation patterns, and the frequency of exponents `≥ 2` is one of them; the block count per Syracuse step would then converge to `1/2` as a frequency. What differs is the parametrisation: converting a rate horizon `θ` to a bottom altitude uses the envelope (5.14)–(5.15) per stage (displayed) and yields `N_0 = N^{1 − 2θ log₂(4/3)}` (§3.3). **Verdict: not delivered; would be delivered in a different time parametrisation, with the conversion supplied by the per-stage statement itself rather than by an extra theorem.**

**(iii) Valuation words versus `(m, r)` letters.** By `13.6.5`'s dictionary, the letter `(m, r)` is the exponent word `(1^{m−1}, 1+r)` and the block boundaries are the renewal times `{a_t ≥ 2}`; so a finite letter pattern is a finite valuation pattern anchored at a renewal time, and its per-block frequency is the ratio of two Syracuse-time pattern frequencies. If every finite valuation pattern has its `Geom(2)` frequency, every finite letter pattern has its `B` frequency. **Verdict: interchangeable at the level the (c)-argument would deliver; nothing delivered past the first window.**

**(iv) The budget-rate clause `τ`.** Tao's proof has no budget clause and no cemetery symbol: it never tallies an orbit past a fresh window's budget, because each scale draws fresh (`N_y`) and the descent below one window is reached through (1.20), not by continuing to read the same start's digits. Inside a window the exponent sum is controlled as a sum by (5.12), which is more than a frequency statement — the content `13.2.3` notes the hypothesis does not supply for `τ ≥ 1`. The (c)-argument would carry (5.12) per stage, so along a rate horizon inside the descent ceiling the number of divisions would track `4 ×` the block count stage by stage, and the clause `S_n < Λ_N` would bind only at `o(T_N)` blocks for every consistent `(τ, θ)` with `θ < 1/β`. **Verdict: no counterpart in Tao; would be satisfied non-vacuously by the per-stage sum control if the (c)-argument were carried out.**

**(v) The two-sided depth marginal `π_{k,D}`.** Tao's 3-adic objects are laws over starts at fixed time: `Syr^n(N) mod 3^k` is within `2^{-c_1 n}` of `Syrac(Z/3^kZ)` under Proposition 1.9's hypotheses (pp. 9–10, via (1.21)–(1.23)), and `13.6.5` already attributes the depth law's 3-adic factor to that object. The along-orbit frequency version — what `π_{k,D}` asserts — is a different statement. By `13.6.3`(iii) the capped depth at a visit is a function of the `W` preceding letters, so it is a finite letter pattern, and by `13.6.4` (⇒) all-pattern letter genericity gives window-state equidistribution against `π_{k,D}` including its depth marginal. **Verdict: not delivered by Tao; reduces to (iii) through the wiki's own theorem.**

Summary: the (c)-argument, if carried out, would deliver `13.2.1`'s letter statistics and, through `13.6.4`, `13.2.2`'s window statistics — at logarithmic density on `[y, y^α]`, altitude-parametrised, with the budget clause satisfied by per-stage sum control. What would remain unfound even then: natural density (Q4), the dyadic-window form and hence `13.2.5`, `13.6.4`'s equivalence as an in-house theorem, and the calibration record.

## 5. Q4 — Remark 1.16, and Inselmann

**Remark 1.16 (p. 12), verbatim.** "In order to upgrade logarithmic density to natural density in our results, it seems necessary to strengthen Proposition 1.14 by establishing a suitable fine scale mixing property of the entire random affine map `Aff_{Geom(2)^n}`, as opposed to just the offset `F_n(Geom(2)^n)`. This looks plausibly attainable from the methods in this paper, but we do not pursue this question here." Remark 1.4 (p. 3) says the same in weaker words: "it is plausible that some refinement of the arguments below will allow one to replace logarithmic density by natural density in the definition of 'almost all'."

**What it would add.** The affine map is `Aff_a(x) = 3^n 2^{-|a|} x + F_n(a)` (1.3). Section 5's computation of `P(Aff_a(N_y) = M)` (p. 25) uses the `1/N` weight of the logarithmic distribution to make the probability of hitting `M` depend on `a` only through `2^{-|a|}` and the 3-adic residue class of `M` (the display after (5.19)); the multiplier `3^{n−m_0} 2^{-|a|}` drops out because `1/N` is invariant under scaling up to the interval's endpoints. With a natural-density-uniform start that cancellation is unavailable, and the joint fine-scale behaviour of multiplier and offset — the "entire random affine map" — would have to be mixed 3-adically. This is the "multiplicative invariance" of p. 2 and the `exp(O(n^{1/2}))` remark of p. 7 in one place: the reason for logarithmic density is exactly the ingredient Remark 1.16 names.

**Inselmann 2024, from the record.** `publication.md` (the landscape item) and `aeh.md` `13.3.2` record: Thm 1.10 and Cor. 1.4, the two-sided envelope `(3/4)^k m^{1±ε}` out to `(log₂(4/3))^{-1} log₂ m` Syracuse steps and descent below `m^ε`, at natural density 1; Thm 1.6, the parity first moment; the technique `∗`-density, "a strengthening of natural density that survives one push-forward". `briefs/v3r3-inselmann-horizon-findings.md` (source read page by page, 2026-08-02) §1.8: "Nowhere in the paper is any pattern of length `≥ 2` in the parity word counted, and no run-length distribution appears. Everything past the classical range is a statement about the running *sum* of parities plus the offset bound `r_k(m)`; the `∗`-density transport (Lemmas 2.13–2.16) transports set membership, not word statistics." And its open item 1: whether the transport lemmas would carry two-letter statistics "is a research question I did not attempt and Inselmann does not address." The record is sufficient; 2402.03276 was not re-read.

**Verdict.** Inselmann supplies the envelope and the first moment at natural density, over one push-forward to altitude `m^ε` — not to `f(m)` for arbitrary `f → ∞`, so not Tao's theorem at natural density either — and no distributional statement. Neither source, and not their combination, gives pattern frequencies past the digit budget at natural density. Remark 1.16's ingredient is not supplied by Inselmann's technique as recorded: `∗`-density is a different route around the non-iteration obstruction (a stronger density notion for the start), not fine-scale mixing of the affine map.

## 6. Q5 — what in Sections 3 and 5 contradicts the brief's reading

The brief's Section 1 reading has the right skeleton — one classical window per scale, glued by Proposition 1.11 whose engine is Proposition 1.14 (p. 12, quoted) — and three specifics wrong or under-specified:

The first-passage law `ν_x` is supported within a factor `exp(O(log^{0.6} x))` of `x` (by (5.14) at `n = T_x`) and is not shown to be, nor used as, near-uniform mod `2^{n'}`; Section 5's whole apparatus (Proposition 5.2, `E'`, `Syrac`, Proposition 1.14) exists because the push-forward's controllable fine structure is 3-adic.
