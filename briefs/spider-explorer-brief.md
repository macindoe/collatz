# Brief: the spider explorer — `viz/spider_explorer.html`, one spider at a time: its abdomen, its legs, each hair down to its last node with the rule that fired at every step, and the borders where it hands over to its parent spider and its child spiders — for a delegated session

## The deliverable: `viz/spider_explorer.html`

One self-contained HTML file in the house style (no external resources of any kind; `BigInt` for every integer; abbreviate integers longer than about 24 digits with click-to-reveal as the comb explorer does; UTF-8 with the charset meta). **The arithmetic core** goes in one `<script>` block delimited by the exact comment lines `// === SPIDER CORE BEGIN ===` and `// === SPIDER CORE END ===`, with no DOM access inside, so it can be run under node. Functions, all on `BigInt` with states as `[Ω, D]`:

- the comb primitives `v2, v3, exitData, stateOfOdd, F, doors, parent, children` exactly as the comb explorer defines them (copy them; the two pages stay independent files);
- `nodeClass(state, parentDepth)` → `abdomen | fork | link | root` from `(Ω, D)`, and the finer labels the comb explorer already uses (`leg`, `hair fork`, `hair link`, `orphan link`) from the parent's depth;
- `owner(state)` → the spider (abdomen state, or the root's piece) that owns a node: descend through depth-1 parents until an abdomen or the root (Theorem 19.2.2);
- `legs(abdomen)` → the list of depth-1 children with, for each, which rule produced it (`cascade`, `door a` with `a`) and the leg's own class;
- `hair(leg)` → the chain `h₁ → h₂ → …` with, at every node, its class, its core's residues `mod 3, 8, 9, 24`, the value `t = v₃(4Ω − 1)` at a fork, and the rule that decided the next step: *link → fork, always* (Lemma 19.1.2); *fork continues iff `t = 1`, and then to a fork iff `Ω ≡ 1 (mod 9)`, a link iff `Ω ≡ 4 (mod 9)`* (Lemma 19.1.3); *fork with `t ≥ 2` ends the hair* — together with the child spiders the node spawns (a fork's cascade child, always an abdomen; its top-door child when `t ≥ 2`);
- `spider(abdomen)` → the whole piece: the abdomen, its legs, its hairs, its size, the child spiders (from the abdomen's own deeper children — the `a = 1` child when `3 | 2^{D+1}Ω − 1` and the top-door child when its depth is `≥ 2` — and from every fork in its hairs), and its parent spider `owner(parent(abdomen))`;
- `selfTest()` with canaries from spiders.md and its findings: the spider of `(1,2)` (its legs, hairs and its **four** child spiders, per the findings' corrected hand-derivation), `(17,1)` an orphan link owned by the spider of its grandparent's owner, `(13,1)` a leg fork of `(5,2)`'s spider with children `(17,2)` and `(17,1)`, `(7,1)` a leg with children `(1,4)` and `(1,3)`, the orphan classes `1, 17, 19 (mod 24)` on `300` deterministic pseudo-random depth-1 states, the `mod 9` continuation rule on `300` forks, and `owner` agreeing with the partition on `300` random nodes (every node's owner is an abdomen or the root, and the owner of a spider's every node is that spider).

**The page.**

1. **Pick a spider.** An input accepting a state `Ω,D` or a positive odd integer `x`; the page computes the node's owner and opens that spider. A state with `D ≥ 2` is its own abdomen. The default on load is the spider of `(1,2)`. Two navigation links on every spider: **parent spider** (the owner of the abdomen's parent; the root's piece for `(1,2)`) and **child spiders**, one link per spawned abdomen, labelled by the node that spawned it and the rule.
2. **The spider, laid out.** The abdomen card first (state, peak, depth, residues `mod 3, 8, 9, 24`, its leg count with the formula `D − 1 + [a=1 child is depth 1] + [top-door child is depth 1]`, its size, its number of hairs and child spiders). Then each leg as the head of a hair, rendered as a **flat row per node** (no nesting; the comb explorer's lesson): the leg's producing rule, then along the hair each node's card — state, class badge, residues, `t` at a fork — with the rule that fired written between consecutive nodes in words and with the lemma number. At the end of each hair, the border: the fork's `t ≥ 2` child spiders as stubs (or, for a hair whose last node is a fork with `t ≥ 2`, both stubs), and for every fork along the way its cascade-child spider stub as well, since every fork spawns one. Stubs are clickable and open that spider.
3. **The borders, summarised.** One small table per spider: each child spider with its abdomen, the spawning node, the rule (`abdomen's a = 1 child`, `abdomen's top-door child`, `hair fork's cascade child`, `hair fork's top-door child, t = …`), and the child's depth. And the parent spider with the edge that connects them (the abdomen's own comb parent and its type, cascade or door).
4. **Reading aids in the record's register.** One paragraph at the top saying what a spider is and what a click does, pointing to spiders.md §19 by lemma number; the registered vocabulary exactly (abdomen, leg, hair, fork, link, orphan link, spider, spider tree); no family-frame vocabulary; flat prose.
5. **Guards.** A spider is finite (hairs end), but hairs can be long (the census saw `55`) and cores grow along a hair; the page renders one spider at a time, flat, and never precomputes neighbours beyond the stubs. A go-to that lands on the root's piece shows `(1,1)` alone with its single child spider `(1,2)`. Refuse `x = 1` gracefully as the root.
6. **Self-test on load.** The footer prints `self-test: N checks, 0 failures` from `selfTest()`.

## Verification: `experiments/spider_explorer_check.py`

Fresh Python, imports nothing from any existing script. It (1) extracts the core between the markers, runs it under node (v20 is installed) with a harness that evaluates the core on inputs read as JSON and prints JSON; (2) implements `owner`, `legs`, `hair`, `spider`, the classes and the rules independently in Python from spiders.md's definitions; (3) compares on the canaries and on `1,000` random abdomens (`Ω < 10^6`, `2 ≤ D ≤ 12`) for the full spider (size, legs with rules, every hair node-by-node with its class and rule, the child-spider list with spawning nodes and rules, the parent spider), on `500` random depth-1 nodes for `owner`, and on `300` random odd `x < 10^12` for the go-to; (4) runs the page under a stubbed `document` for a go-to of `(1,2)`, of `17`, and of a random abdomen with a long hair (search among the random abdomens for the longest hair found and use it), measuring the maximum DOM nesting depth under the page's main container (must be a small stated constant) and the row count against the spider's size; (5) prints per-function counts and `TOTAL: checks = N, failures = 0`. **One documented command reproduces the committed output in full** (`python experiments/spider_explorer_check.py`); foreground; wait; commit the output. If `node` is missing the script must fail, not pass vacuously.
