SUMMARY

Three worst problems
1. Undefined density in the hard sections. The abstract and §§4–6 put six to ten unglossed technical names into single sentences (2-adic logarithm, 3-adic absorption, anchor, window, trichotomy, minimax, extremal profile, exceptional set, density-one, Lean 4). A reader without number theory is lost by p.7 (§4), p.8 (§5) and p.11 (§6). Fix: a plain-words pass where each concept gets one concrete example (the x = 7 orbit is the obvious one), and formula-by-formula statements move out of the main text into the appendix pointers.
2. The mechanism is asserted, not shown, at the joints. The reader is told "so", "therefore", "hence" or "consequently" with no visible reason at the points that matter: "at least 3" (§4), "So q is at most each of these p numbers" (§5), "exactly when s is even" (§4), "the exit depends only on ω and d" (§3), "Nothing is lost … therefore" (§3), the exact 1/3 rate (§6), and "Consequently … intrinsic" (§5). Fix: one line of reasoning or one worked number at each joint.
3. Broken promises and internal inconsistencies. (a) "Symbols at a glance" is placed after §1, yet the text promises each symbol is explained where it first appears; the abstract and §1 use x, T, ω, d, F, p, X0, p1(X0), s, γ before the table. (b) The table claims to cover all symbols of §§1–8 but λ and k appear in §8 and M(ω), v2 and Z_2 in the abstract. (c) "next depth" and "next block's entry depth" are two names for quantities that read as the same (§4). (d) As printed, the staircase example's exit valuations sum to 55, not K = 149 (§5, p.9); please verify the digits against the source. (e) §6 calls the hypothesis a theorem for admissible pairs with budget rate below 1, while §1 and p.4 call it a conjecture, with no reconciliation.
